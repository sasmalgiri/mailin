# Gate 10 pilot kit: five-job usability pilot (2026-11-18)

This kit is for the owner. It runs gate 10 of `FIVE_JOBS_MASTER_PLAN.md`, "Usability pilot (9 of 10 on each task; partial outputs recognised)". You need **10 participants**, **one session each** (about 50–60 minutes), one observer and one test device per session. An agent wrote this kit. It cannot run the pilot, because the gate needs real people. Plan §"Close the human usability gate" says: "The agent prepares fixtures, instructions and scoring; automated tests do not substitute for people."

Files:
- `PILOT_KIT_2026-11-18.md`: this document.
- `PILOT_SCORESHEET_2026-11-18.csv`: the empty score sheet. It has one row per participant × task, for U01–U10.
- Appendix A: the test-archive generator.
- Appendix B: the scorer. Copy each into a file before you use it.

---

## 1. Purpose and pass rule

**Purpose (plan, release gate 10):** "A representative usability pilot completes the five tasks without developer coaching. Initial target: at least 9 of 10 participants succeed on each ordinary task, and all participants correctly identify deliberately partial/unverified outputs and avoid destructive mistakes. This is an internal acceptance target, not a population-wide marketing statistic."

The five tasks come from plan §8.3 ("Real-user task test"):
1. Open a supplied archive and explain whether import is complete.
2. Locate a message in a nested folder.
3. Find a document from text that appears only inside its attachment.
4. Review repeated copies and correctly keep a changed version.
5. Export selected mail and explain whether it is ready to use elsewhere.

For each attempt, record task success, time, wrong selections, missed content, help requests and whether the participant could interpret partial results.

**Pass rule used in this kit.** Gate 10 passes only if all of the following hold on one release-candidate build:

| Rule | Applies to | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary tasks | T1, T2, T3, T4, T5 (each scored separately) | **at least 9 of 10** participants score **S (success)**. **P (partial) does not count as success.** |
| Partial / unverified outputs recognised | R1 (every participant); R2 (iPad / iPhone participants) | **every participant the check applies to** scores S |
| Destructive mistakes | every task | **0** (see the definition in §4) |
| Coaching | every task | none. A task where the observer had to show the way is F. |

SUS and SEQ scores are reported, but they are not part of the pass rule. As the plan says, the result is an internal acceptance target. It is not a claim about all users.

## 2. Participants

**How many:** 10 people who complete the session. Recruit 12 so that one or two no-shows do not leave you short. If someone stops partway, their session is incomplete: replace them, and do not score their partial session.

**Who.** The plan asks for "representative archive users". Aim for this mix:

| Profile | Target count |
|---|---|
| People who keep or hand over mail archives at work: paralegals, litigation support, records / compliance staff | 3–4 |
| Small-business owners or bookkeepers who keep years of mail | 2–3 |
| IT or office admins who move mailboxes between systems | 1–2 |
| Researchers, journalists or family historians who keep old mail | 1–2 |

**Exclude:** anyone who has worked on mailin, seen these tasks, or read this kit. At most 2 participants may have used mailin before. Record that in `observer_notes`.

**Devices:**
- At least **4 on a Mac** and at least **4 on an iPad**.
- Up to 2 may use an iPhone.
- Each participant uses the device type they normally use for mail, if possible.

**Languages:** at least **2 participants run the app in a language other than English**. The app ships English plus 10 languages. The What's New text reads: "English, German, Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese". The plan requires localisations to be updated with the app (§ review notes and translations).
- Set that language on the test device before the session (iPad / iPhone: Settings ▸ General ▸ Language & Region; Mac: System Settings ▸ General ▸ Language & Region, or per app).
- Give the participant translated task cards. The search words (`zanzibarite`, `pelicanfjord`) and file names stay as they are.
- The observer must understand the participant's answers. Use a bilingual observer, or record the answers and have them translated.

**Data.** Participants use **only the supplied test archive**. Plan §8.3 speaks of "permission to use their data". This kit deliberately uses a synthetic archive instead, by owner decision. Record this in the gate-10 evidence as a limitation (§7).

**Consent text.** Read it aloud, then have it signed or ticked. Translate it for non-English participants.

> **Taking part in a mailin usability session**
>
> We are testing the mailin app, not you. You will do a few short tasks with a test archive of made-up emails that we provide. You will **not** use or show any of your own mail, and nothing from your own devices or accounts is opened.
>
> We will note what you do, how long each task takes and what you say, and you will fill in two short questionnaires. Your notes are stored under a code (such as U03), not your name, and are used only to improve mailin. Your name is kept separately and deleted when the study ends.
>
> Screen recording is optional: ☐ I agree to the screen being recorded (no camera, no audio of anyone else) ☐ I do not agree.
>
> You can stop at any time, skip any task, and ask for your notes to be deleted, without giving a reason.
>

## 4. Tasks

**Order:** T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 → T5 → R2 (iPad / iPhone only) → R1, the same for everyone. The tasks build on each other: the archive must be in before anything else, and T4 must undo its change before T5 counts the Inbox. Plan §8.3 suggests comparing with the participant's previous method in counterbalanced order. That comparison is optional and not part of gate 10; §5 asks one question about it.

**Rules for the observer:**
- Hand over one card at a time, read it aloud, and start the timer when you finish reading.
- Stop the timer when the participant says they are done, or at the time limit.
- **Destructive mistake:** the participant ends a task with mail removed that should stay, or with anything permanently deleted. Examples: a changed version moved to the Trash and not brought back; both copies of an email removed; the Trash emptied; "Delete All My Data" or "Clear & Start Fresh" confirmed. Mark `destructive_mistake = Y`; this fails the gate.
  - If the participant notices and undoes it **within the task**, it is not destructive. Record it under `errors`.
- **Recovery after a failed task:** if a task fails and the next task depends on it, put the app in the needed state yourself **after** stopping the timer, using the "Observer recovery" line. Note it in `recovered_by`.

### T1. Open a large archive and say whether it is complete (plan task 1)

**Participant card:**
> Someone has handed you an email archive: the file **FiveJobs.zip** in your Downloads (on iPad / iPhone: in *On My iPad / On My iPhone*). Get it into mailin so you can work with all of it. Make sure nothing is left out, **including repeated copies of the same email**: you will need them later.
> When you are finished, tell me: **did everything come in? How many emails? How do you know?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | 10 min, including the wait while it loads |
| **Success (S)** | The archive is in, with every copy kept. On the receipt, Saved = 7+N and Duplicates = 0, and the folder list shows Work 2. The participant **finds the import receipt** (or the equivalent completion statement) and says it is **complete**, with the count (7+N) or by pointing to the "Complete" verdict. |
| **Partial (P)** | The archive is in with every copy, but the participant cannot say whether it is complete, or says "complete" with no evidence (for example "it stopped moving"). Also P: the right answer is reached only after a help request answered with a neutral prompt. |
| **Fail (F)** | Not imported in the time limit; or repeated copies were skipped (receipt shows a Duplicates / skipped count, and Work shows fewer than 2); or the participant says it is complete when the receipt says Partial / Failed; or the observer had to show the way. |
| **Record** | Time; time spent waiting for the import (separately, in `observer_notes`); which duplicate choice was picked; whether the receipt was found and how (from the finish toast, the history, never); the exact answer; help requests; wrong turns. |
| **Observer recovery** | If repeated copies were skipped: New Import ▸ Clear & Start Fresh (the confirmation shows the count), then import again with "Keep every copy". |

### T2. Folders preserved: find a message in a nested folder (plan task 2)

**Participant card:**
> A colleague says an email introducing **a new client** was filed in a **folder inside the Work folder** of the original archive. Find that email and tell me **the full folder path** and **how many emails are in that folder**. Then tell me whether **every folder of the original archive** came across, including any that has nothing in it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | 4 min |
| **Success (S)** | Opens "Client intro". Names the path **Work ▸ Clients** (any form that shows Clients is inside Work). Says Clients holds **2**. Lists Inbox, Work, Clients, Archive 2019 (and Bulk), and says **Archive 2019 is empty**. |
| **Partial (P)** | Finds the email but gives the path wrongly or incompletely (for example only "Clients"), or misses the empty folder, or gives a wrong count. |
| **Fail (F)** | Does not find the email in the time limit, or finds it only by searching for its subject and cannot say which folder it is in. |
| **Record** | Time; how it was found (folder list or search); the path and counts given; whether the old "Folders / Labels" block (above Source Folders) misled them. GATE1 notes this block as possibly confusing, so quote anything they say about it. |

### T3. Find a document by a word that is only inside an attachment (plan task 3)

**Participant card:**
> One of the emails has an attached document that mentions the word **zanzibarite**. The word is **not in the text of any email**, only inside the attachment. Find the email, **open the document**, and show me the word.
> Then do the same for the word **pelicanfjord**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | 6 min for both words |
| **Success (S)** | For *zanzibarite*: reaches "Board pack" and opens the text of `report.docx` (inside `bundle.zip`), with the word visible. For *pelicanfjord*: reaches "Board pack" and opens `minutes.pdf` or its text, with the word visible. Both done. |
| **Partial (P)** | Finds the right email for both words but cannot open or show the document for one or both; or completes one word only. |
| **Fail (F)** | Finds neither email, or opens a wrong email and says it is the one. |
| **Record** | Time; the search route (main search field, Advanced Search, scrolling); whether the evidence chip ("Inside attachment: report.docx") was noticed and used; wrong selections; missed content. |

### T4. Remove an exact duplicate safely, keep the changed version, then undo (plan task 4)

**Participant card:**
> This archive contains **repeated copies** of some emails. Remove only copies that are **exactly the same**, so that one of each remains. If an email was **later changed**, even slightly, it must **not** be removed.
> Tell me what you removed and what you kept, and why.
> Then **change your mind**: bring back what you removed, so that the archive is as it was before this task.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | 8 min |
| **Success (S)** | Exactly **one** copy of "Quarterly ledger" is removed: the total drops from 7+N to 6+N, and Trash 1. "Travel plans" and "Travel plans (revised)" are **both kept**, and the participant says the revised one differs, so it stays. Then the removed copy is brought back: the total is 7+N again, Trash 0, Inbox 3, Work 2. |
| **Partial (P)** | The removal is correct and both Travel plans emails are kept, but the participant cannot bring the copy back in the time limit (observer restores it). Or everything is done but the participant cannot say why the revised email was kept. |
| **Fail (F)** | Removes nothing; or removes a Travel plans email, or both ledger copies, even if later restored (if not restored by the end of the task, also mark it destructive); or empties the Trash. |
| **Record** | Time; whether the count shown before the move was read ("1 redundant copy will move…"); whether near duplicates were switched on and how the "similar, not identical" group was understood; the reason given; how they undid it (Restore from Trash, Undo, other); destructive mistakes. |
| **Observer recovery** | Restore from the Trash until the total is 7+N and Trash is 0. |

**Participant card:**
> Make a copy of the emails in the **Inbox** folder as a standard email file that another mail program could open, and save it into the **Pilot** folder (Mac: on the Desktop; iPad / iPhone: *On My iPad / On My iPhone ▸ Pilot*).
> Then tell me: **is that copy complete and ready to use elsewhere? How do you know?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | 8 min |
| **Success (S)** | A file (or folder of files) with the **3** Inbox emails is in Pilot. The participant says it is complete and points to evidence. One form of evidence is the receipt's "Complete — 3 written and saved; the saved copy read back identical" (iPad / iPhone) or "Complete — 3 written" (Mac). The other is running the export check: "rehash checked…", "content checked: 3 of 3". |
| **Partial (P)** | Saved correctly, but the participant says "yes" without evidence, or cannot find the receipt. Or the wrong scope was exported (for example the whole archive) but it was saved and checked. |
| **Fail (F)** | Nothing saved in Pilot in the time limit; or the participant says it is complete when the receipt says otherwise (for example "Written, not saved"). |
| **Record** | Time; the format chosen; the scope (Inbox, or other); whether the pre-flight count (3) was read; whether the participant ran the export check without being asked; the exact answer. |

## 5. Observer script and questionnaires

### 5.1 What to say

**Opening** (read it, about 2 min):
> Thank you for coming. We are testing the app, not you. If something is hard, that is what we want to find out. I'll give you a few tasks one at a time on cards. Please work as you normally would, and say out loud what you are looking for and what you expect, if that feels natural. I can't help you during a task, because we want to see how the app works on its own. If you get stuck, tell me, and you can stop the task at any time. There are no wrong answers to my questions afterwards.

**During a task:**
- Neutral prompts only: "What are you looking for?", "What do you expect to happen?", "What would you try next?", "Please tell me when you are done."
- When asked for help: "What would you try?" (count it as a help request). If they are still stuck after a second request, or at the time limit, say "Thank you, let's move on", and mark F.
- Answer factual questions about the task card itself ("Is the Pilot folder on the Desktop?"), but not questions about the app.

**Do not say:**
- any button, menu or screen name: Import, Receipt, Duplicates, Trash, Restore, Advanced Search, Export, Verify, Share, Source Folders;
- "correct", "right", "almost", or a sigh;
- anything about what the app will show.

Do not point, touch the device, or nod at the screen during a task.

**After each task:** stop the timer. Ask the SEQ question (5.2), then "Anything you expected that didn't happen?" Note quotes word for word.

**After the last task:** the SUS form (5.3), then the wrap-up questions (5.4). Thank them, and give any agreed thank-you.

### 5.2 Single Ease Question (after each task)

> **Overall, how difficult or easy was this task?**
> 1 Very difficult · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6 · 7 Very easy

Record it in `seq_score`. For a failed task, still ask.

### 5.3 System Usability Scale (once, at the end)

Each item is rated 1 = Strongly disagree … 5 = Strongly agree:
1. I think that I would like to use this app frequently.
2. I found the app unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the app was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this app.
5. I found the various functions in this app were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this app.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this app very quickly.
8. I found the app very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the app.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this app.

**Score:**
- For each odd item, take the answer − 1.
- For each even item, take 5 − the answer.
- Add them up and multiply by 2.5, which gives 0–100.

Record it in the participant's `SUS` row, `sus_score`.

### 5.4 Wrap-up questions (about 3 min)

1. How do you do these jobs today (tools, steps)? Was any task here quicker or slower than your usual way?
2. Was there a moment you were unsure whether something had **finished**? Which?
3. Was there a moment you worried you might **lose** mail? Which?
4. One thing you would change first?

## Appendix A: `make_pilot_archive.py`

- With `--bulk 0`, the four mailbox files are identical to the gate-1 walk's, apart from the embedded ZIP times.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Pilot test archive for the mailin gate-10 usability pilot.

With --bulk 0 (the default) the output has the layout and content of FiveJobsFixture
(maxmailinUITests/MailinClickThroughUITests.swift, enum FiveJobsFixture)
that GATE1_UI_WALK_2026-11-18.md walked: 7 messages in 4 folders (the
embedded ZIP timestamps are fixed to 2025-01-07 so every run is identical).
With --bulk N a fifth top-level folder "Bulk" holds N extra, distinct
messages so the import and the export take long enough to watch (and to
cancel). Deterministic: the same N always gives the same mailbox contents
(the outer ZIP carries file times, so make it once and reuse that one file).

Usage:  python3 make_pilot_archive.py [--bulk N] [--out DIR]
Result: DIR/FiveJobs.zip  (and DIR/FiveJobs/, the unzipped tree)
"""
import argparse, base64, io, os, random, shutil, subprocess, zipfile

ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
ap.add_argument("--bulk", type=int, default=0)
ap.add_argument("--out", default=".")
args = ap.parse_args()
os.makedirs(args.out, exist_ok=True)
root = os.path.join(args.out, "FiveJobs")
shutil.rmtree(root, ignore_errors=True)
docxWord = "zanzibarite"; pdfWord = "pelicanfjord"

def msg(frm, subj, day, mid, body):
    return (f"From sender@example.com Mon Jan {day:2d} 10:00:00 2025\nFrom: {frm}\nTo: me@example.org\n"
            f"Subject: {subj}\nDate: Mon, {day} Jan 2025 10:00:00 +0000\nMessage-ID: <{mid}@example.com>\n\n{body}\n\n")

def pdf(text):
    content = f"BT /F1 14 Tf 72 700 Td ({text}) Tj ET".encode()
    objs = [b"<< /Type /Catalog /Pages 2 0 R >>", b"<< /Type /Pages /Kids [3 0 R] /Count 1 >>",
            b"<< /Type /Page /Parent 2 0 R /MediaBox [0 0 612 792] /Contents 4 0 R /Resources << /Font << /F1 5 0 R >> >> >>",
            b"<< /Length %d >>\nstream\n" % len(content) + content + b"\nendstream",
            b"<< /Type /Font /Subtype /Type1 /BaseFont /Helvetica >>"]
    out = b"%PDF-1.4\n"; offs = []
    for i, o in enumerate(objs):
        offs.append(len(out)); out += b"%d 0 obj\n" % (i + 1) + o + b"\nendobj\n"
    x = len(out); out += b"xref\n0 %d\n0000000000 65535 f \n" % (len(objs) + 1)
    for o in offs: out += b"%010d 00000 n \n" % o
    out += b"trailer\n<< /Size %d /Root 1 0 R >>\nstartxref\n%d\n%%%%EOF\n" % (len(objs) + 1, x)
    return out

def zipbytes(members):
    b = io.BytesIO()
    with zipfile.ZipFile(b, "w", zipfile.ZIP_STORED) as z:
        for n, d in members: z.writestr(zipfile.ZipInfo(n, date_time=(2025, 1, 7, 9, 0, 0)), d)
    return b.getvalue()

docx = zipbytes([("[Content_Types].xml", '<?xml version="1.0"?><Types xmlns="http://schemas.openxmlformats.org/package/2006/content-types"><Default Extension="xml" ContentType="application/xml"/><Override PartName="/word/document.xml" ContentType="application/vnd.openxmlformats-officedocument.wordprocessingml.document.main+xml"/></Types>'),
                 ("word/document.xml", f'<?xml version="1.0"?><w:document xmlns:w="http://schemas.openxmlformats.org/wordprocessingml/2006/main"><w:body><w:p><w:r><w:t>The audit found {docxWord} in the ledger.</w:t></w:r></w:p></w:body></w:document>')])
bundle = zipbytes([("report.docx", docx)])

def part(name, typ, data):
    b64 = base64.encodebytes(data).decode()
    return f"--FJBOUNDARY\nContent-Type: {typ}; name=\"{name}\"\nContent-Disposition: attachment; filename=\"{name}\"\nContent-Transfer-Encoding: base64\n\n{b64}\n"

att = ("From sender@example.com Tue Jan  7 09:00:00 2025\nFrom: Carol Board <carol@example.com>\nTo: me@example.org\nSubject: Board pack\n"
       "Date: Tue, 7 Jan 2025 09:00:00 +0000\nMessage-ID: <fj-attach@example.com>\nMIME-Version: 1.0\nContent-Type: multipart/mixed; boundary=\"FJBOUNDARY\"\n\n"
       "--FJBOUNDARY\nContent-Type: text/plain; charset=utf-8\n\nThe board pack: minutes and the bundle.\n"
       + part("minutes.pdf", "application/pdf", pdf(f"Board minutes. Action item: {pdfWord} review."))
       + part("bundle.zip", "application/zip", bundle) + "--FJBOUNDARY--\n\n")
ledger = msg("Alice Ledger <alice@example.com>", "Quarterly ledger", 6, "fj-exact", "The quarterly ledger is closed. Totals are final and signed off.")

    p = os.path.join(root, rel); os.makedirs(p, exist_ok=True)
    open(os.path.join(p, "mbox"), "w").write("".join(msgs))
    open(os.path.join(p, "Info.plist"), "w").write('<?xml version="1.0"?><plist version="1.0"><dict/></plist>\n')
    open(os.path.join(p, "table_of_contents"), "wb").write(bytes([0, 1, 2, 3]))

package("Inbox.mbox", [ledger, att, msg("Dave Notes <dave@example.com>", "Plain note", 10, "fj-plain", "Lunch at noon.")])
package("Work.mbox", [ledger, nearA])
package("Work.mbox/Clients.mbox", [nearB, msg("Frank Client <frank@example.com>", "Client intro", 11, "fj-client", "Meet the new client team.")])
package("Archive 2019.mbox", [])

if args.bulk > 0:
    # Distinct filler: unique Message-IDs, random vocabulary, no pilot keywords.
    rng = random.Random(20261118)
    syll = ["ka", "lo", "mi", "ren", "tos", "vel", "dar", "no", "pe", "qui", "sa", "tun", "bri", "go", "hal"]
    months = ["Jan", "Feb", "Mar", "Apr", "May", "Jun", "Jul", "Aug", "Sep", "Oct", "Nov", "Dec"]
    def word(): return "".join(rng.choice(syll) for _ in range(rng.randint(2, 4)))
    bulk = []
    for i in range(args.bulk):
        y = 2018 + i % 6; m = months[(i // 6) % 12]; d = 1 + i % 28
        subj = " ".join(word() for _ in range(rng.randint(2, 5))).capitalize()
        body = " ".join(word() for _ in range(rng.randint(40, 160)))
        bulk.append(f"From bulk@example.net Mon {m} {d:2d} 08:00:00 {y}\nFrom: Sender {i % 97} <s{i % 97}@example.net>\n"
                    f"To: me@example.org\nSubject: {subj}\nDate: Mon, {d} {m} {y} 08:00:00 +0000\n"
                    f"Message-ID: <pilot-bulk-{i}@example.net>\n\n{body}.\n\n")
    package("Bulk.mbox", bulk)

zp = os.path.join(args.out, "FiveJobs.zip")
if os.path.exists(zp): os.remove(zp)
subprocess.run(["/usr/bin/zip", "-q", "-r", "FiveJobs.zip", "FiveJobs"], cwd=args.out, check=True)
print(f"{zp}: {7 + args.bulk} messages in {4 + (1 if args.bulk > 0 else 0)} folders")
```

## Appendix B: `score_pilot.py`

```python
import csv, sys, collections
all_rows = list(csv.DictReader(open(sys.argv[1])))
rows = [r for r in all_rows if r["task_id"] in ("T1", "T2", "T3", "T4", "T5", "R1", "R2")]
people = sorted({r["participant_id"] for r in rows})
by = collections.defaultdict(list)
for r in rows: by[r["task_id"]].append(r)
ok = len(people) == 10
print(f"participants: {len(people)} (10 needed)")
for t in ("T1", "T2", "T3", "T4", "T5", "R1", "R2"):
    rs = [r for r in by[t] if r["outcome"] != "NA"]
    s = sum(r["outcome"] == "S" for r in rs); p = sum(r["outcome"] == "P" for r in rs)
    need = len(rs) if t.startswith("R") else 9     # R: every participant it applies to
    passed = s >= need and (t.startswith("R") or len(rs) == 10) and len(rs) > 0
    ok &= passed
    print(f"{t}: {s} success, {p} partial, {len(rs) - s - p} fail of {len(rs)}; need {need} -> {'PASS' if passed else 'FAIL'}")
d = [r for r in rows if r["destructive_mistake"].strip().upper() == "Y"]
print(f"destructive mistakes: {len(d)} -> {'PASS' if not d else 'FAIL'}"); ok &= not d
sus = [float(r["sus_score"]) for r in all_rows if r["task_id"] == "SUS" and r["sus_score"]]
if sus: print(f"SUS mean {sum(sus) / len(sus):.1f} (n={len(sus)}; reported, not part of the pass rule)")
print("GATE 10:", "PASS" if ok else "FAIL")
```
